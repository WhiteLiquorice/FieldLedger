# FieldLedger hood-cleaning outreach kit

Status: prepared for review. Local implementation and live release evidence are separate. Start outreach using the demo only after its deployed URL and download flow pass the release gate. Accept real customer data only after the authenticated production gates pass. No messages have been sent.

## Initial customer and offer

Buyer: an owner or operations manager at a kitchen exhaust / hood-cleaning company with one to three field technicians. Start with teams whose photos and service notes currently arrive through texts, email, or paper and require office reconstruction.

Offer: $49/month for up to three technicians, with a 14-day workspace trial. The first trial should cover one actual customer site and a complete service visit. Record what the operator needs changed before expanding to more sites.

Product promise: keep a visit's system identity, before-and-after photos, observations, and exceptions together in a downloadable customer PDF. This is a record of operator-entered work. It does not establish that inaccessible ductwork was cleaned or independently certify compliance.

Do not claim customer adoption, measured time savings, inspector acceptance, avoided disputes, insurance protection, or replacement of an existing dispatch/invoicing system without specific evidence. FieldLedger does not currently send customer reports automatically or generate invoices.

## First conversation

Ask about the last completed visit, rather than hypothetical interest:

1. How did the technician get the site instructions?
2. Where did the before-and-after photographs end up?
3. Who assembled the customer record, and what was missing?
4. What happens when a duct section cannot be accessed?
5. How is the next visit scheduled?
6. Would you try this on one site with one technician? Who decides whether to pay?

With permission, ask for a redacted example report. Record the operator's own wording and the exact follow-up they agree to.

## Ten-minute demonstration

- 0–2 minutes: ask how their last report was assembled.
- 2–4 minutes: open the explicitly fictional hood-cleaning demo; show the customer site and exhaust system, then the assigned stop.
- 4–7 minutes: start service, enter cleaned areas, attach labeled before-and-after photos, and explain how to record an exception.
- 7–9 minutes: finalize and download the PDF. Open the downloaded file and inspect its photographs.
- 9–10 minutes: ask what would prevent their crew from using this on one site. Offer the trial only when live onboarding is verified.

Always reset the fictional demo between prospects. Do not put real customer details in the public demo.

## Common questions

**We already have job-management software.** Ask whether their current service record is sufficient. Show the export and let them assess whether a second tool is worthwhile. Do not quote unverified competitor pricing or promise an integration.

**Does this work offline?** Open the stop while connected. Draft checklist entries and pending photos are saved on that device during interruptions. Reconnect to upload and finalize. Workspace loading and authentication require a connection; signing out clears local drafts.

**Does this certify the cleaning?** No. It records the provider's observations and evidence. The provider remains responsible for the work, procedures, and professional judgment.

**Can the customer receive the report?** The office downloads the PDF and sends it through its existing process. Automated report email is not part of the initial offer.

## First trial success criteria

- Owner verifies email, creates the workspace, and imports or enters a customer site.
- Technician accepts the invitation and sees the assigned stop on their actual phone.
- One real visit is recorded with usable before-and-after evidence or an explained exception.
- Office opens the PDF and confirms the customer, system, photos, notes, and dates are correct.
- Operator states whether the record fits their process and whether they will continue at $49/month.
